# Community Benefit Agreement — Emergency Power Sharing Clause

**Directive 37 | June 2026 | For June 8 Whitepaper**
**Monroe Campus — Union County, NC**

---

## Preamble

This Community Benefit Agreement (CBA) is entered into between **[Developer Entity]**, the developer and operator of the Monroe Data Center Campus (the "Project"), and the **City of Monroe, North Carolina** (the "City"), to ensure that the Project serves as a net benefit to the local community and electrical grid. This Agreement supplements the Project's conditional use permit and is recorded against the property.

---

## Article III: Grid Emergency Power Sharing

### Section 3.1 — Capacity Commitment

The Developer shall install, maintain, and operate **no less than 5 megawatts (MW) of dispatchable emergency power capacity** available to the City of Monroe for use during declared grid emergency events. This capacity shall be:

- Derived from the Project's on-site generation assets (the MPG30000 turbine array or battery energy storage system)
- Separately metered at the point of common coupling with the City's distribution system
- Capable of islanded operation independent of the Duke Energy transmission grid

### Section 3.2 — Triggering Events

The City may call upon the emergency power capacity when any of the following conditions are met:

1. **Duke Energy declares a grid emergency** (EEA-2 or higher per NERC Standard EOP-002)
2. **City-wide blackout or brownout** affecting > 10% of Monroe municipal service territory
3. **Extreme weather event** (NWS-issued hurricane warning, ice storm, or heat emergency) where grid supply is compromised
4. **Public safety power shutoff** ordered by the City Manager or Emergency Management Director
5. **Voluntary dispatch** during peak demand events (June–September, 3PM–7PM) to reduce strain on local substations

### Section 3.3 — Dispatch Protocol

| Step | Action | Responsible Party | Timeframe |
|------|--------|------------------|-----------|
| 1 | City notifies Developer of emergency event | City Emergency Ops | Immediate |
| 2 | Developer confirms availability and synchronizes to City distribution | Developer Ops | 15 minutes |
| 3 | Power flows to designated critical infrastructure feeders | Automated switchgear | 20 minutes |
| 4 | Full 5MW dispatched to City grid | Developer Ops | 30 minutes |
| 5 | Continuous status updates; termination upon event resolution | Both parties | Ongoing |

### Section 3.4 — Priority Feeder Designation

The 5MW emergency capacity shall be pre-wired to serve the following **critical municipal infrastructure** in priority order:

1. **Monroe Water Treatment Plant** — ensures water pressure and fire flow continuity
2. **Monroe Regional Fire Station #1** — emergency response operations
3. **Union County Emergency Operations Center** — situational command
4. **Monroe City Hall** — administrative continuity
5. **Union County Sheriff's Office** — public safety communications
6. **Remaining capacity for residential lifeline loads** (selected via City-managed circuit assignment)

### Section 3.5 — Compensation

| Dispatch Type | Rate | Cap |
|--------------|------|-----|
| Grid emergency (unplanned) | $0.00/kWh (donated) | First 24 hours per event |
| Extended emergency (>24h) | Cost recovery: fuel + O&M | Not to exceed $0.15/kWh |
| Peak demand (voluntary) | Market rate + 10% | Per ISO/RTO settlement |

**Emergency power dispatched under Section 3.2 (1–4) for the first 24 hours is donated at no cost to the City or to ratepayers.**

### Section 3.6 — Infrastructure Requirements

The Developer shall, at its sole cost:

- Install a dedicated 5MW feeder from the Project substation to the City's nearest distribution node
- Install automatic synchronization switchgear (grid-forming capable)
- Deploy a dedicated 5MW-hour battery buffer (lithium-iron-phosphate, 4-hour duration) to enable seamless transition during grid separation
- Install revenue-grade metering at the interconnection point
- Maintain a dedicated fiber link between Project control room and City Emergency Operations Center

### Section 3.7 — Term and Testing

| Obligation | Frequency | Standard |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Load bank test | Monthly | 5MW for 30 minutes |
| Grid islanding test | Quarterly | Full disconnect/reconnect cycle |
| City emergency drill | Annually | Coordinated with Union County Emergency Management |

The Developer's obligation under this Agreement runs with the land for the **full operational life of the Project**, including any subsequent owner or operator.

### Section 3.8 — Non-Performance

If the Developer fails to deliver emergency power during a verified event under Section 3.2:

- **First occurrence**: Notice of non-compliance; cure period of 30 days
- **Second occurrence within 12 months**: Liquidated damages of $50,000, paid to City general fund
- **Third occurrence within 24 months**: The City may petition the NC Utilities Commission to revoke the Project's standby generation permit

Force majeure (fuel supply interruption, equipment destruction, natural disaster) exempts the Developer from non-performance penalties.

## Article VII: Additional Community Benefits

### Section 7.1 — Workforce Development

The Developer shall:
- Fund a **$500,000 annual scholarship program** for Union County Public Schools STEM students
- Partner with South Piedmont Community College on a **Data Center Operations Certificate** program
- Guarantee **60% local hire** for operational roles (within 30-mile radius)

### Section 7.2 — Infrastructure Fund

The Developer shall contribute **$100,000 per year** to the Monroe Municipal Infrastructure Fund for road maintenance, broadband expansion, and emergency services equipment upgrades.

### Section 7.3 — Public Transparency

The Developer shall maintain a live public dashboard displaying:
- Campus power consumption (MW)
- Emergency power availability and battery state of charge
- Water consumption (zero-evaporation verified)
- Noise monitoring data (property line + nearest residence)
- Emergency dispatch events and power delivered to City
